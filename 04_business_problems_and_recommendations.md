HR Analytics – Business Problems and Recommendations



1\. Project Objective



The objective of this project is to analyse employee attrition data using MySQL, identify employee groups with higher attrition rates, understand workforce patterns, and provide recommendations to improve employee retention.



2\. Identified Business Problems



Problem 1: High Attrition Among Entry-Level Employees



Key findings:

\- Employees at Job Level 1 who work overtime: 52.56% attrition.

\- Employees with 0–2 years of total work experience: 43.90%.

\- Employees under 25 years of age: 39.18%.



Business interpretation:



The company experiences higher attrition among early-career and entry-level employees.



Recommendations:

\- Improve onboarding and training programmes.

\- Provide mentoring and career guidance.

\- Review workloads assigned to new employees.

\- Create opportunities for professional development.



Problem 2: High Attrition in Sales



Key findings:

\- Sales department attrition: 20.63%.

\- Sales Representative attrition: 39.76%.

\- Research and Development attrition: 13.84%.

\- Human Resources department attrition: 19.05%.



Business interpretation:



The Sales department has a higher attrition rate than the other departments. Sales Representatives have the highest attrition rate among the analysed job roles.



Recommendations:

\- Review sales targets and performance expectations.

\- Evaluate incentive and reward structures.

\- Provide additional support to sales employees.

\- Investigate workload and employee feedback.



Problem 3: Overtime and Employee Attrition



Key findings:

\- Employees working overtime: 30.53% attrition.

\- Employees not working overtime: 10.44% attrition.



Business interpretation:



Employees who work overtime have a substantially higher attrition rate than employees who do not.



Recommendations:

\- Monitor overtime frequency.

\- Distribute workloads more effectively.

\- Review staffing requirements.

\- Encourage a healthier work-life balance.



Problem 4: Low Employee Satisfaction



Key findings:

\- Job satisfaction level 1: 22.84% attrition.

\- Work-life balance level 1: 31.25%.

\- Environment satisfaction level 1: 25.35%.



Business interpretation:



Employees reporting low satisfaction levels have higher attrition rates in these categories.



Recommendations:

\- Conduct regular employee feedback surveys.

\- Improve workplace conditions.

\- Review employee workload and work-life balance.

\- Develop employee engagement initiatives.



Problem 5: Career Development and Employee Retention



Key findings:



Employees with job satisfaction level 1 and 2–4 years since their last promotion had a 27.50% attrition rate.



Business interpretation:



Low job satisfaction combined with a longer wait since the last promotion may be associated with higher attrition in this group.



Recommendations:

\- Review career progression opportunities.

\- Provide relevant training programmes.

\- Communicate promotion criteria clearly.

\- Discuss career goals during employee reviews.



3\. Overall Business Recommendations



Based on the analysis, the company should prioritise:



1\. Improving retention among entry-level employees.

2\. Investigating high attrition in the Sales department.

3\. Monitoring overtime and workload distribution.

4\. Improving employee satisfaction and work-life balance.

5\. Strengthening career development opportunities.



4\. Conclusion



This project used MySQL to clean, validate and analyse employee data containing 1,470 records.



The analysis identified several employee groups with higher attrition rates, particularly entry-level employees, Sales Representatives and employees working overtime.



These findings can help HR teams identify areas that require further investigation and develop employee retention strategies.



Note: The analysis identifies associations and patterns in the available data. It does not establish that any single factor directly causes employee attrition. Further investigation using employee feedback, exit interviews and additional HR data would be needed to determine the underlying causes.
